# Sosban Fach (Little Saucepan)
## Traditional Welsh Folk Song

---

## About the Song
A humorous traditional Welsh folk song, particularly associated with the Llanelli area and Llanelli RFC (Scarlets rugby team). The song tells the story of domestic chaos with a boiling saucepan, a crying cat, and a baby in a cradle. It's upbeat, fun, and often sung at rugby matches!

---

## Full Lyrics with Phonetics and Translation

### Verse 1

**Mae bys Meri-Ann wedi brifo,**
*(My bees Meh-ree-ANN weh-dee BREE-voh)*
*Mary-Ann's finger is hurt,*

**A Dafydd y gwas ddim yn iach.**
*(Ah DAV-ith uh gwass thim un EE-akh)*
*And David the servant is not well.*

**Mae'r baban yn y crud yn crio,**
*(My'r BAB-an un uh kreed un KREE-oh)*
*The baby in the cradle is crying,*

**A'r gath wedi scrapo Joni bach.**
*(Ah'r gath weh-dee SKRA-poh JON-ee bahkh)*
*And the cat has scratched little Johnny.*

---

### Chorus (Repeated often!)

**Sosban fach yn berwi ar y tân,**
*(SOSS-ban vahkh un BER-wee ar uh tan)*
*Little saucepan boiling on the fire,*

**Sosban fawr yn berwi ar y llawr,**
*(SOSS-ban vowr un BER-wee ar uh llour)*
*Big saucepan boiling on the floor,*

**A'r gath wedi scrapo Joni bach.**
*(Ah'r gath weh-dee SKRA-poh JON-ee bahkh)*
*And the cat has scratched little Johnny.*

---

### Verse 2

**Dai bach y sowldiwr,**
*(Die bahkh uh SOWL-jeer)*
*Little Dai the soldier,*

**Dai bach y sowldiwr,**
*(Die bahkh uh SOWL-jeer)*
*Little Dai the soldier,*

**Dai bach y sowldiwr,**
*(Die bahkh uh SOWL-jeer)*
*Little Dai the soldier,*

**A gwn ar ei ysgwydd.**
*(Ah goon ar eye UH-sgoo-ith)*
*With a gun on his shoulder.*

---

### Verse 3

**Mae bys Meri-Ann wedi gwella,**
*(My bees Meh-ree-ANN weh-dee GWEL-ah)*
*Mary-Ann's finger has healed,*

**A Dafydd y gwas yn ei fedd;**
*(Ah DAV-ith uh gwass un eye veth)*
*And David the servant is in his grave;*

**Mae'r baban yn y crud wedi tyfu,**
*(My'r BAB-an un uh kreed weh-dee TUH-vee)*
*The baby in the cradle has grown,*

**A'r gath wedi huno mewn hedd.**
*(Ah'r gath weh-dee HIN-oh mewn heth)*
*And the cat has slept in peace (died peacefully).*

## Rugby Version (Llanelli Scarlets)

At rugby matches, fans often sing a shortened, more energetic version:

**Sosban fach yn berwi ar y tân,**
**Sosban fawr yn berwi ar y llawr,**
**A'r gath wedi scrapo Joni bach!**

Then immediately repeat with increasing volume and enthusiasm!

Many fans also add actions and movements, especially during the "scrapo" part!

---

## Quick Singing Guide

### Pronunciation Tips:
- **Sosban** (SOSS-ban) - Like "sauce" + "ban"
- **Fach/Bach** (vahkh/bahkh) - Soft "ch" like in "loch"
- **Berwi** (BER-wee) - "Boiling" - emphasize the first syllable
- **Scrapo** (SKRA-poh) - Borrowed from English "scrape"
- **Tân** (tan) - Long "ah" sound, means "fire"

### Key Words:
- **Sosban fach** = Little saucepan (feminine adjective)
- **Sosban fawr** = Big saucepan (mutation of "mawr")
- **Y gath** = The cat
- **Joni bach** = Little Johnny (term of endearment)

### Rhythm Notes:
- It's a lively, bouncy tune
- The chorus is the most important part
- Perfect for group singing
- Often gets faster with each repetition at rugby matches

---

## Performance Tips:

### At Rugby Matches:
1. Start slow and build momentum
2. Clap along to the beat
3. Get louder with each chorus
4. Some fans make scratching motions during "scrapo"
5. Link arms and sway during slower verses

### General Singing:
1. Keep it light-hearted and fun
2. Emphasize the rhyming words
3. The darker verse 3 is often skipped in festive settings
4. Children love the silly story of domestic chaos

---

## Cultural Notes:
- Extremely popular in Llanelli and surrounding areas
- The official song of Llanelli RFC (Scarlets rugby team)
- Origins are unclear, but likely 19th century
- The story is deliberately absurd and humorous
- "Sosban" is Welsh for saucepan/small pot
- Often sung at weddings, parties, and celebrations
- Verse 3's dark humor (death) is typical of traditional Welsh folk songs

---

## Fun Facts:
- There's a sculpture of a sosban in Llanelli town center
- The Scarlets rugby team is nicknamed "Sospan" or "The Sospans"
- Different regions have slightly different versions
- Some versions have additional verses
- The tune is instantly recognizable across Wales
