# Calon Lân (A Pure Heart)
## Traditional Welsh Hymn

---

## About the Song
One of the most beloved Welsh hymns, "Calon Lân" is about the importance of having a pure heart over worldly riches. Written by Daniel James (Gwyrosydd) with music by John Hughes in 1891. It's sung at rugby matches, funerals, weddings, and is deeply emotional for Welsh people. Often brings tears to eyes when sung by crowds.

---

## Full Lyrics with Phonetics and Translation

### Verse 1

**Nid wy'n gofyn bywyd moethus,**
*(Nid win GOV-in BUH-wid MOYTH-iss)*
*I don't ask for a luxurious life,*

**Aur y byd na'i berlau mân:**
*(Eye-r beed nah'ee BER-lie mahn)*
*The gold of the world nor its fine pearls:*

**Gofyn 'rwy am galon hapus,**
*(GOV-in roi am GAL-on HAP-iss)*
*I ask for a happy heart,*

**Calon onest, calon lân.**
*(KAL-on ON-est, KAL-on lahn)*
*An honest heart, a pure heart.*

---

### Chorus (Cytgan)

**Calon lân yn llawn daioni,**
*(KAL-on lahn un llown die-OH-nee)*
*A pure heart full of goodness,*

**Tecach yw na'r lili dlos:**
*(TEK-akh yoo nah'r LIL-ee dloss)*
*Is fairer than the pretty lily:*

**Dim ond calon lân all ganu,**
*(Dim ond KAL-on lahn all GAN-ee)*
*Only a pure heart can sing,*

**Canu'r dydd a chanu'r nos.**
*(KAN-eer deeth ah KHAN-eer noss)*
*Sing the day and sing the night.*

---

### Verse 2

**Pe dymunwn olud bydol,**
*(Peh duh-MIN-oon OL-id BUH-dol)*
*If I desired worldly wealth,*

**Chwim adenydd iddo sydd;**
*(Khwim ad-EN-ith ITH-oh seeth)*
*It has swift wings;*

**Golud calon lân, rinweddol,**
*(GOL-id KAL-on lahn, rin-WETH-ol)*
*The wealth of a pure, virtuous heart,*

**Yn dwyn bythol elw fydd.**
*(Un doo-in BUH-thol EL-oo veeth)*
*Will bring eternal profit.*

---

### Verse 3

**Hwyr a bore fy nymuniad**
*(Hoo-ir ah BOR-eh vuh nuh-MIN-ee-ad)*
*Evening and morning my desire*

**Gwyd i'r nef ar adain cân,**
*(Gweed eer nehv ar AD-ine kahn)*
*Rises to heaven on the wing of a song,*

**Ar i Dduw, er mwyn fy Ngheidwad,**
*(Ar ee Thee, er moin vuh NKHAY-dwad)*
*For God, for the sake of my Saviour,*

**Roddi i mi galon lân.**
*(ROTH-ee ee mee GAL-on lahn)*
*To give me a pure heart.*

## Quick Singing Guide

### Pronunciation Tips:
- **Calon** (KAL-on) - Means "heart," emphasis on first syllable
- **Lân** (lahn) - Long "ah" sound, means "pure/clean"
- **Llawn** (llown) - That unique Welsh "ll" sound + "own"
- **Daioni** (die-OH-nee) - "Goodness," three syllables
- **Tecach** (TEK-akh) - "Fairer," soft "ch" ending
- **Lili** (LIL-ee) - "Lily," both "i"s sound like "ee"

### Key Vocabulary:
- **Calon lân** = Pure/clean heart
- **Aur** = Gold
- **Perlau** = Pearls
- **Hapus** = Happy
- **Onest** = Honest
- **Canu** = To sing
- **Dydd** = Day
- **Nos** = Night

### Rhythm Notes:
- It's a hymn, so sing with reverence and feeling
- Take your time - don't rush
- The chorus should be powerful and emotional
- Often sung slowly at emotional moments
- Can be sung faster at rugby matches

---

## Performance Tips:

### Emotional Singing:
1. This is a song from the heart - sing with feeling
2. The chorus is the most powerful part
3. "Calon lân yn llawn daioni" should crescendo
4. Many people get emotional during this song - it's normal
5. In crowds, the sound is incredibly moving

### At Different Events:

**Rugby Matches:**
- Sung faster and with more energy
- Crowds often sway together
- Very powerful when sung by thousands

**Funerals/Memorials:**
- Sung slowly and reverently
- Often brings tears
- Celebrates the "pure heart" of the deceased

**Weddings:**
- Joyful but emotional
- Celebrates the love and purity of the couple

**Eisteddfodau:**
- Performed with great vocal skill
- Can be very elaborate arrangements

## Cultural Significance:
- One of Wales' most treasured songs
- Represents Welsh values: humility, honesty, purity
- Often the last song sung at major events
- Regularly voted Wales' favorite hymn
- The message resonates across generations
- Non-religious people still love it for its message
- It's about character over materialism - a very Welsh value

---

## Famous Performances:
- Sung by 74,000+ at Wales rugby matches in Principality Stadium
- Performed at rugby legend Phil Bennett's funeral
- Sung on the pitch after major Welsh rugby victories
- Often performed by Welsh choirs internationally
- Featured in films and documentaries about Wales

---

## Why It's Beloved:
✓ Beautiful, timeless melody
✓ Message about purity and goodness
✓ Works in both happy and sad contexts
✓ Unites Welsh people emotionally
✓ Simple but profound lyrics
✓ Perfect for communal singing
✓ Represents core Welsh values

---

## Musical Notes:
- Written in 3/4 time (waltz time)
- Key of F major (often)
- Four-part harmony is traditional
- The melody rises and falls beautifully
- Easy to harmonize for choirs

---

## Translation Philosophy:
The title "Calon Lân" is better translated as "A Pure Heart" rather than "Clean Heart." It's about moral purity, innocence, and goodness - the idea that spiritual wealth matters more than material wealth. This theme runs through much of Welsh culture and values.

## Practice Exercise:
1. Start by listening to a recording (search YouTube for "Calon Lân Wales rugby")
2. Learn the chorus first - it's the emotional core
3. Practice "Calon lân yn llawn daioni" slowly and clearly
4. The word "calon" appears frequently - master it!
5. Build up verse by verse
6. Try singing along with crowd recordings for the full experience
