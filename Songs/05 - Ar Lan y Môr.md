# Ar Lan y Môr (On the Seashore)
## Traditional Welsh Love Song

---

## About the Song
A beautiful traditional Welsh love song, "Ar Lan y Môr" is one of the most romantic songs in the Welsh language. It tells the story of love by the seaside. Often sung at weddings and romantic occasions, it has a gentle, lilting melody that's perfect for learning Welsh pronunciation. Great for beginners because of its repetitive structure.

## Quick Singing Guide

### Pronunciation Tips:
- **Ar lan y môr** (Ar lan uh mohr) - "On the seashore" - this phrase starts every line!
- **Môr** (mohr) - Long "oh" sound, means "sea"
- **Mae** (my) - "There is/are" - sounds like "my"
- **Rhosys** (RHOSS-uss) - "Roses" - roll that "rh"!
- **Cochion** (KOKH-ee-on) - "Red" (plural)
- **Gwynion** (GWIN-ee-on) - "White" (plural)
- **'Nghariad** (NGHAR-ee-ad) - "My love/sweetheart"
- **Cysgu** (KUSS-goo) - "Sleeping"
- **Codi** (KOD-ee) - "Rising/getting up"

### Key Vocabulary:
- **Ar lan** = On the shore/edge
- **Y môr** = The sea
- **Rhosys** = Roses
- **Lilis** = Lilies
- **Carreg** = Stone
- **Blodau** = Flowers
- **Gwynt** = Wind
- **Nos** = Night
- **Bore** = Morning

### Pattern Recognition:
Notice the beautiful repetitive structure:
- Every line starts with "Ar lan y môr mae..." (On the seashore there is/are...)
- Every verse ends with the same two lines about sleeping and waking
- This repetition makes it perfect for learning!

2. **Flowing melody** like gentle waves
3. **Not too fast** - let the beauty of the words shine

## Grammar Points to Notice:

### Mutations:
- **cariad** → **'nghariad** (my love) - nasal mutation after "fy"
- **môr** stays the same after "y" (the)
- **codi** → **chodi** - aspirate mutation after "a" (and)

### Useful Phrases:
- **mae... mae... mae...** (there is/are) - notice how this creates rhythm
- **Ar lan y** = on the edge of the
- **'r nos** = the night (shortened from "y nos")
- **'r bore** = the morning (shortened from "y bore")
