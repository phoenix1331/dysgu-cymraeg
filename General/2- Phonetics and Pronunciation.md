# Welsh Phonetics and Pronunciation

> [!tip] Practice Makes Perfect
> Welsh pronunciation follows consistent rules. Once you learn them, reading Welsh becomes much easier!

## The Welsh Alphabet

The Welsh alphabet has 29 letters (including 8 digraphs):
a, b, c, ch, d, dd, e, f, ff, g, ng, h, i, j, l, ll, m, n, o, p, ph, r, rh, s, t, th, u, w, y

## Vowels

### Simple Vowels
- **a** - like "ah" in "father" (e.g., cath = CAH-th = cat)
- **e** - like "eh" in "bed" (e.g., te = TEH = tea)
- **i** - like "ee" in "see" (e.g., ti = TEE = you)
- **o** - like "o" in "hot" (e.g., dog = DOG = document)
- **u** - like "ee" in "see" in South Wales, "ih" in North Wales
- **w** - like "oo" in "boot" (e.g., cwm = KOOM = valley)
- **y** - two sounds:
  - like "uh" in "but" in final syllables (e.g., dyn = DIN = man)
  - like "ee" in "see" elsewhere (e.g., y = uh = the)

## Consonants (Special Welsh Sounds)

### Ch
- Like the "ch" in Scottish "loch" or German "Bach"
- **NOT** like "ch" in "cheese"
- Examples:
  - chi = KHEE (you - formal)
  - coch = KOKH (red)

### Dd
- Like "th" in "this" or "that"
- Examples:
  - dydd = DEETH (day)
  - da = DAH (good), dda = THAH (good - mutated)

### F
- Like "v" in "van"
- Examples:
  - fach = VAKH (small - feminine)
  - fi = VEE (me)

### Ff
- Like "f" in "fat"
- Examples:
  - ffôn = FOHN (phone)
  - coffi = KOH-fee (coffee)

### Ll
- No direct English equivalent
- Place tongue as if saying "L", then blow air around sides
- Like a breathy "HL" sound
- Examples:
  - llan = HLAN (church)
  - Llangollen = hlan-GOH-hlen

### Rh
- A breathy "R" sound
- Like "hr" at the start
- Examples:
  - rhad = HRAD (cheap)

### Si/Si/Sî
- "S" followed by "i" sounds like "sh"
- Examples:
  - siarad = SHAH-rad (to speak)
  - siop = SHOP (shop)

## Common Phonetic Patterns

### Double Consonants
- Double consonants are usually pronounced the same as single, but may affect vowel length

### Stress
- Usually on the second-to-last (penultimate) syllable
- Examples:
  - Cymraeg = cum-RIGH-g
  - diolch = dee-OLKH

## Tips for Learning

1. Listen to native speakers on Duolingo and other resources
2. Practice the "ch" and "ll" sounds regularly - they're uniquely Welsh
3. Remember: "w" and "y" are vowels in Welsh
4. "f" is always "v", "ff" is always "f"
5. Stress is usually on the second-to-last syllable
