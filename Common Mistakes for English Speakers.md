<a name="grammar-mistakes"></a>
## 1. Grammar Mistakes

### **Mistake #1: Using "yn" before verb-nouns with "bod"**

❌ **Wrong:** "Dw i yn mynd" / "Dw i yn hoffi coffi"
✅ **Correct:** "Dw i'n mynd" / "Dw i'n hoffi coffi"

**Why:** The "yn" contracts to "'n" before verb-nouns with bod. Never use the full "yn" in this construction.

**Exception:** "Dw i yn y tŷ" (I am in the house) - here "yn" means "in" (location), not the verb marker.

---

### **Mistake #2: Forgetting the apostrophe in contractions**

❌ **Wrong:** "Dw in mynd" / "Mae en dda"
✅ **Correct:** "Dw i'n mynd" / "Mae'n dda"

**Why:** The apostrophe shows the contraction of "yn" to "'n". Without it, the sentence is incomplete.

---

### **Mistake #3: Using "a" for "and" with mutations incorrectly**

❌ **Wrong:** "bara a caws" (should mutate!)
✅ **Correct:** "bara a chaws" (bread and cheese)

**Why:** "A" (and) causes aspirate mutation when followed by words beginning with C, P, or T.

**Examples:**
- "te a choffi" (tea and coffee)
- "tad a phwdin" (father and pudding)
- "ci a thŷ" (dog and house)

---

### **Mistake #4: Using "i" (to/for) without mutations**

❌ **Wrong:** "Dw i'n mynd i Cymru"
✅ **Correct:** "Dw i'n mynd i Gymru" (I'm going to Wales)

❌ **Wrong:** "Rhoi i fi"
✅ **Correct:** "Rhoi i fi" (Give to me - no mutation with pronouns!)

**Why:** "I" causes soft mutation with nouns but NOT with pronouns.

---

### **Mistake #5: Confusing "ei" (his) and "ei" (her)**

❌ **Wrong:** "ei car" without context
✅ **Correct:**
- "ei gar e" (his car - soft mutation)
- "ei char hi" (her car - aspirate mutation)

**Why:** "Ei" means both "his" and "her" - only the mutation tells you which!

**Tip:** Add "e/fe/fo" (him) or "hi" (her) after the noun to clarify.

<a name="mutation-errors"></a>
## 2. Mutation Errors

### **Mistake #6: Forgetting nasal mutations with "fy" (my)**

❌ **Wrong:** "fy tad" (my father)
✅ **Correct:** "fy nhad"

❌ **Wrong:** "fy car"
✅ **Correct:** "fy nghar"

**Why:** "Fy" always causes nasal mutation.

**Common Nasal Mutations:**
| Original | Mutated |
|----------|---------|
| tad → nhad | car → nghar |
| pen → mhen | brawd → mrawd |
| ci → nghi | gardd → ngardd |

---

### **Mistake #7: Over-mutating (mutating when you shouldn't)**

❌ **Wrong:** "Dw i'n gweld gar" (I see a car)
✅ **Correct:** "Dw i'n gweld car"

**Why:** Not every word mutates! Mutations have specific triggers.

**When NOT to mutate:**
- After direct object (except with "bod")
- After "a" (interrogative "does/is")
- After numbers (except 2)
- In many other contexts

---

### **Mistake #8: Confusing soft and aspirate mutations**

❌ **Wrong:** "ei gar hi" (her car - should be aspirate!)
✅ **Correct:** "ei char hi"

❌ **Wrong:** "ei char e" (his car - should be soft!)
✅ **Correct:** "ei gar e"

**Remember:**
- **"ei" (his)** → soft mutation (C→G, P→B, T→D)
- **"ei" (her)** → aspirate mutation (C→CH, P→PH, T→TH)

---

### **Mistake #9: Not mutating after "yn" (predicative/adverbial)**

❌ **Wrong:** "Mae hi'n canu" (she's singing)
✅ **Correct:** "Mae hi'n ganu" (wait, both are actually acceptable!)

**Actually:** Verb-nouns after "yn" often don't mutate in modern Welsh. This one's tricky!

**But these DO mutate:**
- "Mae hi'n **dda**" (she's good - "da" → "dda")
- "Mae'r tŷ'n **fawr**" (the house is big - "mawr" → "fawr")

---

### **Mistake #10: Forgetting compound mutations**

❌ **Wrong:** "yn fy tŷ" (in my house)
✅ **Correct:** "yn fy nhŷ" (nasal + possible soft = nasal wins)

**Why:** Two mutation triggers can occur together - usually the first one applies.

<a name="false-friends"></a>
## 3. False Friends (Words That Look Similar to English But Aren't)

### **False Friend #1: "plant"**

🇬🇧 **English:** plant (vegetation)
🏴󐁧󐁢󐁷󐁬󐁳󐁿 **Welsh:** plant = **children**

**Correct Welsh for "plant" (vegetation):** "planhigyn" (singular) / "planhigion" (plural)

---

### **False Friend #2: "car"**

🇬🇧 **English:** car (vehicle)
🏴󐁧󐁢󐁷󐁬󐁳󐁿 **Welsh:** car = **car** ✅ (This one's actually the same!)

**But note:** Welsh borrowed from English relatively recently.

---

### **False Friend #3: "coffi"**

🇬🇧 **English:** coffee
🏴󐁧󐁢󐁷󐁬󐁳󐁿 **Welsh:** coffi = **coffee** ✅ (same)

**But:** "cofio" = **to remember** (not coffee-related!)

---

### **False Friend #4: "bara"**

🇬🇧 **English:** bar (place)
🏴󐁧󐁢󐁷󐁬󐁳󐁿 **Welsh:** bara = **bread**

**Welsh for "bar":** "bar" (borrowed from English)

---

### **False Friend #5: "pobl"**

🇬🇧 **English:** "people" (sounds similar)
🏴󐁧󐁢󐁷󐁬󐁳󐁿 **Welsh:** pobl = **people** ✅ (actually related!)

---

### **False Friend #6: "hwn"**

🇬🇧 **English:** "one" (sounds similar)
🏴󐁧󐁢󐁷󐁬󐁳󐁿 **Welsh:** hwn = **this** (masculine)

**Welsh for "one":** "un"

---

### **False Friend #7: "pam"**

🇬🇧 **English:** "pam" (not a word)
🏴󐁧󐁢󐁷󐁬󐁳󐁿 **Welsh:** pam = **why**

---

### **False Friend #8: "dim"**

🇬🇧 **English:** dim (not very bright)
🏴󐁧󐁢󐁷󐁬󐁳󐁿 **Welsh:** dim = **no, nothing, not**

---

<a name="pronunciation-pitfalls"></a>
## 4. Pronunciation Pitfalls

### **Mistake #11: Pronouncing "ll" as "l"**

❌ **Wrong:** "llan" (sounds like English "lan")
✅ **Correct:** Place tongue as if saying "L", blow air out the sides - "hlan" (voiceless L)

**Practice words:**
- **llan** (church) - "HLAN"
- **llaeth** (milk) - "HLYKH"
- **llyfr** (book) - "HLUH-veer"

**Tip:** It's like the sound in "athlete" or a cat hissing!

---

### **Mistake #12: Pronouncing "ch" as English "ch"**

❌ **Wrong:** "bach" pronounced like "batch"
✅ **Correct:** "bach" - "BAHKH" (like Scottish "loch")

**Practice words:**
- **bachgen** (boy) - "BAHKH-gen"
- **coch** (red) - "KOKH"
- **nos dda** (good night) - "nohs KHAH"

**Tip:** It's the German "Bach" or Scottish "loch" sound!

---

### **Mistake #13: Pronouncing "f" as "f"**

❌ **Wrong:** "fi" pronounced like "fee"
✅ **Correct:** "fi" - "vee" (like English "V")

**Why:** Welsh "f" = English "v"
Welsh "ff" = English "f"

**Examples:**
- **coffi** (coffee) - "KOV-ee" (v sound)
- **afal** (apple) - "AV-al" (v sound)
- **ffrind** (friend) - "FRIND" (f sound)

---

### **Mistake #14: Stressing wrong syllable**

❌ **Wrong:** "Cymraeg" stressed on first syllable
✅ **Correct:** "CymRAEG" - stress on second-to-last

**General Rule:** Welsh stresses the **second-to-last syllable** (penultimate)

**Examples:**
- **Cymru** - "CUHM-ree"
- **Cymraeg** - "cuhm-RYEG"
- **Aberystwyth** - "ab-er-UHST-with"

**Exceptions:** Monosyllabic words, some borrowings

---

### **Mistake #15: Pronouncing "dd" as "d"**

❌ **Wrong:** "dydd" pronounced like "deed"
✅ **Correct:** "dydd" - "deeth" (like English "th" in "this")

**Practice words:**
- **dydd** (day) - "DEETH"
- **ddoe** (yesterday) - "THOY"
- **Ddyfed** (name) - "THUH-ved"

**Tip:** It's the voiced "th" in "this", not the voiceless "th" in "thin"!

<a name="word-order-confusion"></a>
## 5. Word Order Confusion

### **Mistake #16: Using English word order for questions**

❌ **Wrong:** "Ble ti wyt yn byw?" (English: Where are you living?)
✅ **Correct:** "Ble wyt ti'n byw?" (question word + verb + subject)

**Pattern:** Question Word + Verb + Subject + Rest

**Examples:**
- **Beth wyt ti'n wneud?** (What are you doing?)
- **Pwy mae e?** (Who is he?)
- **Pryd mae'r trên yn cyrraedd?** (When does the train arrive?)

---

### **Mistake #17: Adjective before noun (English style)**

❌ **Wrong:** "mawr tŷ" (big house)
✅ **Correct:** "tŷ mawr" (noun + adjective)

**Pattern:** Noun + Adjective (opposite of English!)

**Examples:**
- **car coch** (red car)
- **bachgen bach** (small boy)
- **merch dda** (good girl)

**Exception:** A few adjectives (hen, prif, hoff) come BEFORE the noun:
- **hen ddyn** (old man)
- **prif ddinas** (main city)

---

### **Mistake #18: Literal translation of English "there is/are"**

❌ **Wrong:** "Yno yw..." (there is...)
✅ **Correct:** "Mae..." / "Mae 'na..." (there is/are)

**Examples:**
- **Mae cath yn yr ardd** (There's a cat in the garden)
- **Mae 'na bobl yma** (There are people here)
- **Oes...?** (Is there...?)

---

<a name="preposition-problems"></a>
## 6. Preposition Problems

### **Mistake #19: Direct translation of "I need"**

❌ **Wrong:** "Dw i angen"
✅ **Correct:** "Mae angen arna i" (lit: "There is need on me")

**Pattern:** Mae angen ar + person

**Examples:**
- **Mae angen arna i help** (I need help)
- **Mae angen arnat ti gwsg** (You need sleep)
- **Mae angen arno fe arian** (He needs money)

---

### **Mistake #20: Using wrong preposition**

❌ **Wrong:** "Dw i'n byw *ar* Cymru"
✅ **Correct:** "Dw i'n byw *yn* Nghymru" / "yng Nghymru" (I live in Wales)

**Common preposition pairs:**
- **yn** (in) - location
- **ar** (on) - surface
- **i** (to) - direction
- **o** (from) - origin
- **gyda/â** (with) - accompaniment

---

### **Mistake #21: Not using prepositional pronouns**

❌ **Wrong:** "Dw i'n mynd gyda ti" (going with you)
✅ **Correct:** This is actually correct! But also: "Dw i'n mynd gyda ti"

**But for "arna i" (on me - I need):**
❌ **Wrong:** "Mae angen ar fi"
✅ **Correct:** "Mae angen arna i"

**Learn these forms:**
- **arna i, arnat ti, arno fe, arni hi** (on me, on you, on him, on her)
- **gyda fi, gyda ti, gyda fe, gyda hi** (with me, with you, with him, with her)

---

<a name="verb-errors"></a>
## 7. Verb Errors

### **Mistake #22: Using "Dw i" for past tense**

❌ **Wrong:** "Dw i'n mynd ddoe" (I go yesterday)
✅ **Correct:** "Es i ddoe" or "Roeddwn i'n mynd ddoe" (I went yesterday)

**Why:** "Dw i" is present tense only!

**Correct past tenses:**
- **Simple past:** Es i, Gest ti, Aeth e/hi
- **Imperfect:** Roeddwn i, Roeddet ti, Roedd e/hi

---

### **Mistake #23: Confusing "wedi" and "mynd i"**

❌ **Wrong:** "Dw i mynd i weld" (I have seen - WRONG!)
✅ **Correct:**
- **Dw i wedi gweld** (I have seen - perfect)
- **Dw i'n mynd i weld** (I'm going to see - future)

**Remember:**
- **wedi** = have done (completed action)
- **mynd i** = going to do (future intention)

---

### **Mistake #24: Forgetting verb-noun mutations**

❌ **Wrong:** "Dw i wedi mynd"
✅ **Correct:** "Dw i wedi mynd" (no mutation after "wedi")

**But:**
❌ **Wrong:** "Dw i'n mynd i mynd"
✅ **Correct:** "Dw i'n mynd i fynd" (soft mutation after "i")

---

<a name="gender-mistakes"></a>
## 8. Gender Mistakes

### **Mistake #25: Ignoring grammatical gender**

Welsh nouns have gender (masculine or feminine), which affects:
- Article mutations
- Adjective mutations
- Pronoun choice

**Feminine nouns cause soft mutation after "y" (the):**
- **y gath** (the cat - "cath" is feminine)
- **y ferch** (the girl - "merch" is feminine)

**Masculine nouns don't:**
- **y bachgen** (the boy - "bachgen" is masculine)
- **y ci** (the dog - "ci" is masculine)

**Common mistake:** Not learning gender with nouns.
**Solution:** Always learn nouns with their gender!

---

<a name="over-translation"></a>
## 9. Over-Translation (Translating Too Literally)

### **Mistake #26: Translating idioms literally**

❌ **Wrong:** "Dw i'n cael fy mhen wedi torri" (I'm getting my head broken)
✅ **Correct:** Use Welsh idioms, not English ones translated!

**Welsh has its own idioms:**
- **Mae hi'n bwrw hen wragedd a ffyn** (It's raining old ladies and sticks = raining cats and dogs)
- **Codi pac** (to pick up pack = to leave)
- **Torri gwynt** (to break wind = to fart - same as English!)

---

### **Mistake #27: Using "have got" structure**

❌ **Wrong:** "Dw i wedi cael car" (I have got a car - sounds weird!)
✅ **Correct:** "Mae car gyda fi" / "Mae gen i gar" (There's a car with me = I have a car)

**Pattern:** Mae [thing] gyda/gan [person]

---

### **Mistake #28: Translating "to be" always as "bod"**

❌ **Wrong (sometimes):** Treating all English "to be" sentences the same
✅ **Correct:** Welsh has different constructions:
- **Identity:** "Fi yw Dafydd" (I am Dafydd)
- **State:** "Dw i'n hapus" (I am happy)
- **Location:** "Dw i yn y tŷ" (I am in the house)
- **Possession:** "Mae car gyda fi" (I have a car)

<a name="self-correction-strategies"></a>
## 10. Self-Correction Strategies

### **Strategy #1: Record yourself speaking**

- Listen for pronunciation mistakes
- Check stress patterns
- Notice if you're pausing too much (might indicate lack of fluency)

---

### **Strategy #2: Read aloud daily**

- Forces correct pronunciation
- Builds muscle memory
- Helps internalize word order

---

### **Strategy #3: Keep a "mistake diary"**

- Note errors you make repeatedly
- Review weekly
- Track improvement over time

---

### **Strategy #4: Use correction apps/websites**

- **Duolingo:** Instant feedback
- **Say Something in Welsh:** Structured correction
- **iTalki:** Native speaker correction

---

### **Strategy #5: Find a language partner**

- Native speaker can correct naturally
- Don't be afraid to make mistakes!
- Ask for specific feedback on recurring errors

---

### **Strategy #6: Shadow native speakers**

- Listen to Welsh radio/TV
- Repeat what they say immediately
- Mimic pronunciation, intonation, rhythm

---

### **Strategy #7: Focus on one mistake type at a time**

- **Week 1:** Focus only on mutations
- **Week 2:** Focus only on pronunciation
- **Week 3:** Focus on word order
- Etc.

Don't try to fix everything at once!

---

### **Strategy #8: Understand WHY you made the mistake**

- **Is it because:** Welsh doesn't work like English here?
- **Is it because:** You don't understand the grammar rule?
- **Is it because:** You're translating too literally?
- **Is it because:** You forgot/didn't learn this pattern?

Understanding the cause helps prevent repetition.

---

### **Strategy #9: Celebrate progress, not perfection**

- Native speakers make mistakes too!
- Communication > perfection
- If you're understood, you're succeeding
- Fluency comes with time

---

### **Strategy #10: Use Welsh in real situations**

- Real-world use reveals mistakes
- Immediate feedback from native speakers
- Forces you to adapt and self-correct
- Builds confidence

## Quick Reference: Top 10 Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Using full "yn" before verb-nouns** → Use "'n"
2. **Forgetting nasal mutations with "fy"** → fy mrawd, fy nhad
3. **Pronouncing "ll" as "l"** → It's voiceless lateral!
4. **Pronouncing "f" as "f"** → It's "v"!
5. **English word order in questions** → Verb before subject
6. **Adjective before noun** → Noun first!
7. **"Dw i angen"** → "Mae angen arna i"
8. **Ignoring grammatical gender** → Learn it with the noun
9. **Confusing "ei" (his) and "ei" (her)** → Check the mutation
10. **Over-translating idioms** → Learn Welsh expressions

## Final Advice

**Making mistakes is essential to learning.**

- Native Welsh speakers will appreciate your effort
- They don't expect perfection
- They'll often gently correct you
- Don't let fear of mistakes stop you speaking!

**The best learners:**
- Make lots of mistakes
- Learn from them
- Keep speaking anyway
- Have a sense of humor about errors

**Peidiwch â phoeni am gamgymeriadau - maen nhw'n normal!**
*(Don't worry about mistakes - they're normal!)*

**Dal ati!**
*(Keep at it!)*
