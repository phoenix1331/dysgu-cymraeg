# Yma o Hyd (Still Here)
## Modern Welsh Anthem of Survival

---

## About the Song
Written and performed by Dafydd Iwan in 1983, "Yma o Hyd" has become an unofficial Welsh anthem. It celebrates the survival of the Welsh language and culture despite centuries of attempts to suppress it. The song's title means "Still Here" - a defiant statement of Welsh identity and resilience. It's incredibly powerful when sung by crowds, especially at rugby matches.

## Rugby Stadium Version (Simplified)

At rugby matches, fans often sing a shortened, high-energy version focusing on the chorus:

**Ry'n ni yma o hyd! (×2)**
**Er gwaetha pawb a phopeth! (×3)**
**Ry'n ni yma o hyd!**

This is repeated over and over, getting louder and more passionate each time!

## Quick Singing Guide

### Pronunciation Tips:
- **Yma o hyd** (UH-ma oh heed) - "Still here" - THE key phrase
- **Ry'n ni** (Reen nee) - "We are" - contraction of "Rydyn ni"
- **Er gwaetha** (Er GWAY-tha) - "Despite" - strong emphasis
- **Pawb** (powb) - "Everyone" - rhymes with "cow"
- **Popeth** (POP-eth) - "Everything"
- **Macsen** (MAK-sen) - Refers to Magnus Maximus, Roman emperor

### Key Vocabulary:
- **Yma** = Here
- **O hyd** = Still/always
- **Er gwaetha** = Despite
- **Cofio** = To remember
- **Storm** = Storm
- **Gwynt** = Wind
- **Môr** = Sea
- **Brwydrau** = Battles

### Rhythm Notes:
- Strong, defiant, marching rhythm
- The chorus is anthemic and powerful
- Perfect for crowd singing
- Often sung with fists raised
- Gets progressively louder and more energetic

## Historical Context:
- **Macsen/Magnus Maximus**: 4th-century Roman emperor who left Britain to claim the imperial throne in Rome. Welsh legend says he married a Welsh princess and his departure left a power vacuum. He represents the idea that invaders come and go, but the Welsh remain.

- **1,600 years**: The song references the time since Macsen, emphasizing the ancient continuity of Welsh culture

- **"Y Treiglo"** (The Mutations/Changes): Historical attempts to suppress Welsh language and culture, particularly through education policies

---

## Why This Song Matters:

### Historical Suppression:
- Welsh language was banned in schools (Welsh Not/Welsh Knot policy)
- Children were punished for speaking Welsh
- English authorities tried to erase Welsh culture
- Road signs, official documents all in English only
- "Wales for the Welsh" movement in 20th century

### Modern Resurgence:
- Welsh is now an official language in Wales
- All children learn Welsh in school
- Bilingual signs everywhere
- Welsh language media (S4C TV channel)
- "Yma o Hyd" celebrates this survival and resurgence
