# Welsh Discussion Practice

Welcome to your Welsh discussion practice folder! These notes are designed to help you practice everyday conversations in Welsh with a partner.

## How to Use These Notes

1. **Find a Practice Partner**: These discussions work best with another person learning Welsh
2. **Take Turns**: Alternate between Person A and Person B roles
3. **Read Aloud**: Practice pronunciation by reading the dialogues out loud
4. **Adapt and Improvise**: Use the dialogues as templates, but fill in your own information
5. **Repeat Regularly**: Practice the same dialogue multiple times to build fluency
6. **Mix and Match**: Combine elements from different dialogues to create new conversations

## Discussion Topics

1. **Greetings and Introductions** - Basic hellos, meeting new people, farewells
2. **Talking About Your Day** - Morning routines, daily activities, evening plans
3. **Food and Drink** - Cafés, restaurants, cooking, food preferences
4. **Weather and Seasons** - Daily weather, seasonal activities, preferences
5. **Family and Friends** - Talking about loved ones, making plans, describing people
6. **Hobbies and Interests** - Sports, arts, music, creative pursuits, gaming
7. **Work and Study** - Jobs, daily work routines, career goals, learning
8. **Shopping and Errands** - Shops, markets, online shopping, running errands
9. **Travel and Transport** - Commuting, public transport, holidays, directions
10. **Health and Wellbeing** - Feelings, illness, healthy habits, fitness

## Tips for Effective Practice

### Before You Start
- Review basic vocabulary for the topic
- Have a dictionary handy (geiriadur.ac.uk is excellent)
- Don't worry about perfection - the goal is communication

### During Practice
- Speak at a natural pace, don't rush
- If you don't know a word, try to describe it in Welsh
- Make mistakes! They're part of learning
- Ask your partner "Sut mae dweud...?" (How do you say...?)
- Use gestures to help communicate

### After Practice
- Note down new words you learned
- Try to use new phrases in future conversations
- Record yourself to hear your progress
- Review difficult sections

## Mutation Guide

Welsh uses mutations (treigladau) - consonant changes in certain contexts. Here are some common patterns you'll see in these dialogues:

- **Soft Mutation** (after 'yn', 'dy', 'fy', etc.):
  - c → g (car → gar)
  - p → b (pen → ben)
  - t → d (tŷ → dŷ)
  - g → (disappears)
  - b → f (brawd → frawd)
  - d → dd (diwrnod → ddiwrnod)
  - m → f (mam → fam)

- Don't stress too much about mutations at first - focus on communication!

## Useful Phrases for Discussion Practice

- **I don't understand**: Dw i ddim yn deall
- **Can you repeat that?**: Alli di ailadrodd hynny?
- **More slowly**: Yn arafach
- **How do you say...?**: Sut mae dweud...?
- **What does that mean?**: Beth mae hynny'n ei olygu?
- **Can you help me?**: Alli di helpu fi?
- **I don't know**: Dw i ddim yn gwybod
- **That's interesting!**: Mae hynny'n ddiddorol!

## Setting Goals

Consider setting conversation goals:
- **Week 1-2**: Complete greetings and daily routine dialogues
- **Week 3-4**: Practice food, weather, and family topics
- **Week 5-6**: Work through hobbies, work, and shopping
- **Week 7-8**: Master travel and health discussions
- **Ongoing**: Mix all topics and create original conversations

## Additional Practice Ideas

1. **Role Play**: Create scenarios (at a restaurant, meeting a friend, etc.)
2. **Question Chains**: One person asks questions, the other answers and asks back
3. **Topic Challenges**: Talk about a topic for 2-3 minutes without stopping
4. **Record Sessions**: Listen back to hear your progress
5. **WhatsApp/Text**: Continue conversations in Welsh between practice sessions
