# Welsh Mutations (Treigladau)

> [!warning] Most Important Grammar Rule
> Mutations are THE most distinctive feature of Welsh grammar. The first letter of a word changes depending on what comes before it. Don't panic - patterns emerge with practice!

## What Are Mutations?

Mutations are changes to the beginning sound of words. Welsh has three types:
1. **Soft Mutation** (Treiglad Meddal) - most common
2. **Nasal Mutation** (Treiglad Trwynol)
3. **Aspirate Mutation** (Treiglad Llaes)

> [!tip] Don't Memorize Everything at Once
> Learn the patterns gradually as you encounter them. Focus on soft mutations first - they're by far the most common.

## Soft Mutation (Treiglad Meddal)

### The Changes

| Original | Mutates to | Example |
|----------|------------|---------|
| c | g | **c**ath → **g**ath (cat) |
| p | b | **p**en → **b**en (head) |
| t | d | **t**ŷ → **d**ŷ (house) |
| g | (disappears) | **g**ardd → ardd (garden) |
| b | f | **b**ag → **f**ag (bag) |
| d | dd | **d**wy → **dd**wy (two-f) |
| ll | l | **ll**yfr → **l**yfr (book) |
| m | f | **m**am → **f**am (mother) |
| rh | r | **rh**ad → **r**ad (cheap) |

### When Soft Mutation Happens

#### 1. After "yn" (in/being)

```
Dw i'n mynd
I am going

cath → yn gath
yn y gath = in the cat
```

#### 2. After Feminine Singular Nouns (with adjectives)

```
côt goch (red coat) - côt is feminine, so coch → goch
car coch (red car) - car is masculine, stays coch
```

#### 3. After "dy" (your - informal)

```
car → dy gar (your car)
mam → dy fam (your mother)
tad → dy dad (your father)
```

#### 4. After "ei" (his)

```
car → ei gar (his car)
tŷ → ei dŷ (his house)
pen → ei ben (his head)
```

#### 5. After Numbers (2, 3, 4)

```
dau gi (two dogs) - ci → gi
tair cath (three cats) - cath → gath
pedwar bachgen (four boys) - bachgen → fachgen
```

#### 6. After "i" (to/for)

```
mynd → mynd i Gaerdydd (going to Cardiff)
Caerdydd → Gaerdydd
```

#### 7. After Question Words

```
Beth? → Beth wnest ti? (What did you do?)
gwneud → wnest
```

#### 8. After Prepositions (am, ar, at, gan, heb, i, o, dan, dros, trwy, wrth)

```
dan y bwrdd (under the table)
bwrdd → bwrdd (b → f... wait, should be "fwrdd" but often stays)
ar y bwrdd (on the table)
```

## Nasal Mutation (Treiglad Trwynol)

### The Changes

| Original | Mutates to | Example |
|----------|------------|---------|
| c | ngh | **c**ath → **ngh**ath |
| p | mh | **p**en → **mh**en |
| t | nh | **t**ŷ → **nh**ŷ |
| g | ng | **g**ardd → **ng**ardd |
| b | m | **b**ag → **m**ag |
| d | n | **d**ydd → **n**ydd |

### When Nasal Mutation Happens

#### After "fy" (my)

```
car → fy nghar (my car)
pen → fy mhen (my head)
tad → fy nhad (my father)
brawd → fy mrawd (my brother)
dillad → fy nillad (my clothes)
```

#### After "yn" (in) before place names

```
Cymru → yng Nghymru (in Wales)
Bangor → ym Mangor (in Bangor)
```

## Aspirate Mutation (Treiglad Llaes)

### The Changes

| Original | Mutates to | Example |
|----------|------------|---------|
| c | ch | **c**ath → **ch**ath |
| p | ph | **p**en → **ph**en |
| t | th | **t**ŷ → **th**ŷ |

> [!note] Only Three Letters
> Aspirate mutation ONLY affects c, p, and t (they add an 'h')

### When Aspirate Mutation Happens

#### 1. After "ei" (her)

```
car → ei char (her car)
pen → ei phen (her head)
tŷ → ei thŷ (her house)
```

#### 2. After "â" (with), "gyda" (with), "tua" (towards)

```
cath → â chath (with a cat)
```

#### 3. After "tri" / "tair" (three) with feminine nouns

```
tair cath → tair gath (soft mutation)
BUT:
tri chi → tri chi (no mutation - masculine)
```

## Practice Examples

### Soft Mutation

```
cath (cat)
y gath = the cat (after y)
dy gath = your cat
ei gath = his cat
dwy gath = two cats

tŷ (house)
y tŷ = the house
dy dŷ = your house
ei dŷ = his house

bag (bag)
y bag = the bag
dy fag = your bag
ei fag = his bag
```

### Nasal Mutation

```
car (car)
fy nghar = my car

pen (head)
fy mhen = my head

tad (father)
fy nhad = my father
```

### Aspirate Mutation

```
car (car)
ei char = her car

pen (head)
ei phen = her head

tŷ (house)
ei thŷ = her house
```

## Why Do Mutations Exist?

Historically, mutations happened because of sounds flowing together in speech. They became part of Welsh grammar over centuries.

Think of it like English: "a" vs "an" (a car / an apple). Welsh just does it more extensively!

## Learning Strategy

1. **Start with soft mutations** - they're the most common
2. **Learn "fy/dy/ei"** - these are used constantly
3. **Notice patterns** as you read and listen
4. **Don't stress perfection** - native speakers understand context
5. **Practice with common words** first

## Common Mistakes

### "My" vs "Your" vs "His" vs "Her"

| Welsh | Mutation | Example | English |
|-------|----------|---------|---------|
| fy | Nasal | fy **ngh**ar | my car |
| dy | Soft | dy **g**ar | your car |
| ei (his) | Soft | ei **g**ar | his car |
| ei (her) | Aspirate | ei **ch**ar | her car |

> [!warning] All four look similar!
> Context and the mutation tell you which one it is.
