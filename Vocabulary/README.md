# Welsh Vocabulary Collection

> [!info] Thematic Learning Approach
> This vocabulary collection is organized by topic to help you learn Welsh words in meaningful contexts. Each topic includes practical words and phrases you'll use in daily life in Wales.

---

## Purpose of This Collection

The thematic vocabulary modules are designed to:

1. **Build practical vocabulary** for real-world situations
2. **Organize words by context** making them easier to remember
3. **Include essential grammar information** (gender, mutations, usage)
4. **Provide pronunciation guides** for every word
5. **Support daily conversation** in Wales

Unlike a simple dictionary, these modules:
- Group related words together
- Show words in context with example phrases
- Highlight common mutations and patterns
- Include regional variations where relevant
- Provide cultural notes and practical tips

## How to Use These Vocabulary Files

### For Beginners

1. **Start with one topic at a time** - Don't try to memorize everything at once
2. **Focus on the most common words first** - Look for frequency indicators
3. **Learn with the pronunciation** - Say the words out loud
4. **Use flashcards or spaced repetition** - Create cards from the tables
5. **Practice in context** - Use the example phrases provided

### For Intermediate Learners

1. **Review systematically** - Go through one module per week
2. **Focus on gaps** - Skip words you already know well
3. **Practice gender and mutations** - These are crucial for fluency
4. **Use words in sentences** - Create your own examples
5. **Learn related expressions** - Study the phrase sections thoroughly

### For Advanced Learners

1. **Refine your vocabulary** - Pick up less common words
2. **Master regional variations** - Compare North and South Welsh terms
3. **Study idiomatic expressions** - Go beyond literal translations
4. **Practice active recall** - Test yourself without looking
5. **Integrate with other resources** - Use with conversation practice

## Learning Strategies for Vocabulary

### Memory Techniques

**1. Chunking**
Group words into categories:
- Foods by type (fruits, vegetables, meats)
- Household items by room
- Actions by context (cooking, cleaning, traveling)

**2. Association**
Connect Welsh words to:
- English cognates (where they exist)
- Visual images
- Personal experiences
- Sound patterns

**3. Spaced Repetition**
- Review new words after 1 day
- Review again after 3 days
- Review again after 1 week
- Review again after 1 month
- Continue with longer intervals

**4. Active Usage**
- Write shopping lists in Welsh
- Label items in your home
- Describe your day in Welsh
- Create mental sentences while cooking, cleaning, etc.

### Gender Learning Tips

Every Welsh noun has a gender (masculine or feminine). This matters because:
- Adjectives mutate after feminine nouns
- Possessive pronouns cause different mutations
- "The" (y/yr/'r) remains the same but following adjectives change

**Strategies:**
1. **Always learn nouns with their gender** - Don't learn "car" learn "car (m)"
2. **Look for patterns** - Some endings indicate gender (though not always!)
3. **Practice with adjectives** - "The big car" = Y car mawr (m) vs Y gath fawr (f)
4. **Use color-coding** - Blue for masculine, red for feminine (in your own notes)

### Mutation Practice

Mutations are systematic sound changes. In vocabulary learning:
- Note which words commonly mutate (after "yn", "i", possessives, etc.)
- Practice the mutated forms, not just the base form
- Use the example phrases to see mutations in context
- Review the [[9- Welsh Mutations|mutations guide]] regularly

## Table Format Reference

All vocabulary modules use consistent table formatting:

| Column | Information | Example |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Welsh** | The Welsh word | afal |
| **Gender** | m (masculine) or f (feminine) | m |
| **Pronunciation** | Phonetic guide | AH-val |
| **English** | English translation | apple |
| **Notes** | Usage notes, plurals, mutations | pl: afalau |

### Reading the Tables

**Gender Markers:**
- **m** = masculine (masc.)
- **f** = feminine (fem.)
- **(v)** = verb-noun (doesn't have grammatical gender)
- **(adj)** = adjective
- **(phrase)** = phrase or expression

**Pronunciation Guide:**
- CAPITALS = stressed syllable
- Hyphens separate syllables
- Common sounds:
  - "th" = like English "thin"
  - "dd" = like English "then"
  - "ll" = breathy "l" (blow while saying "l")
  - "ch" = like Scottish "loch"

## Regional Variations

Welsh has two main dialectal areas: **North Wales (Gogledd)** and **South Wales (De)**.

Vocabulary differences are noted in the modules where relevant:

- **Gyda fi** (South) vs **Gen i** (North) - "I have"
- **Nawr** (South) vs **Rŵan** (North) - "Now"
- **Moyn** (South) vs **Eisiau** (North) - "Want"

Don't worry too much about this as a learner - both forms are understood everywhere in Wales. Choose one variety to focus on (usually based on where you live or which resources you use), but be aware of common alternatives.
